# Agent guide

## Project

Siege of Greywatch is a Django-served, browser-playable isometric siege prototype. `TODO.md` tracks the implementation plan; the current playable milestone includes movement through the open gate, adjacent melee combat, and courtyard capture.

## Target design and remaining work

- Provide a game editor for creating single games and linking them to scenarios.
- Store editable game definitions on the server. Single games now own editable maps and rosters; active instances snapshot the selected definition, and scenarios link to available single games.
- Extend the current move-point/turn rules with morale and promotion to higher ranks with more points. Units now have health and fight points; adjacent melee attacks and defender counterattacks are implemented.
- Support elevation levels and movement between them via ladders and ramps. Exactly one opposing enemy side has soldiers, moves on end turn, and automatically attacks: soldiers with swords use adjacent melee and bow/crossbow carriers use ranged attacks through clear lines of sight with reload cooldowns. Successful ranged attacks return transient projectile events for browser animation; these are not persisted in game state.
- `game/enemy_ai.py` runs defender AI on the server using `http://127.0.0.1:11435/v1` and model `unsloth/Qwen3.8-27B-GGUF:UD-Q4_K_XL`. AI coordinates are goals; the engine routes defenders to legal destinations without entering the objective, and retries malformed orders once. The server reads `SPLASH_API_KEY` from `.env`; never send it to the client, log it, or commit it. An unavailable or persistently invalid AI response leaves the turn unchanged.

## Where to work

- `setup/` contains Django settings and project URLs; `game/` contains catalog models, views, URLs, migrations, and Django tests.
- `game/templates/game/game.html` is the launch/menu/battlefield page; `game/static/game/game.css` styles it.
- `game/static/game/game.js` coordinates server snapshots, actions, and battlefield status; `renderer.js` draws the battlefield, `input.js` handles camera, pointer and keyboard input, and `launch.js` owns the launch screens and catalog. Animation frames do not advance game state.
- `game/engine.py` owns editable map geometry, both rosters, pathfinding, move-point rules, turns, and capture. `GameInstance` persists state and a revision in SQLite; the current session controls access to the active instance.
- Catalog entries are exposed at `/api/scenarios/` and `/api/single-games/`. Migration `game/migrations/0001_initial.py` seeds an entry in each catalog. Starting a match uses `POST /api/games/<kind>/<entry_id>/start/`; `GET /api/games/current/` resumes it; `POST /api/games/actions/` applies a revisioned action. POST requests require CSRF.
- Authenticated staff edit single-game map JSON (cols, rows, keep, gate, objective, startPositions, enemyStartPositions) and link games to scenarios in `/admin/`. Starting positions specify a `weapon` (`sword`, `longbow`, `crossbow`) and optional `tools` list (up to two labels); soldiers have no unit type. Migration `0010` converts saved legacy maps, matches, and history; imported old maps/states are also normalized on edit, launch, resume or action. Older maps without enemyStartPositions spawn one defender near the objective; existing active matches keep their earlier state until restarted. A scenario launch selects a linked game with `single_game_id` in its JSON POST body; a sole linked game can be launched with `{}`. New instances snapshot the map so later edits do not change active matches.
- Maps may include `elevation`: unique open `{x, y, height, access?}` tiles, height 1-3 and optional access `ladder` or `ramp`. A height change of one is legal when the higher tile has access, or when an unmounted soldier carries `rope`; height gaps of two or more remain impassable. Same-height steps are free of special restrictions. No elevation means a flat legacy map. Units store their current tile height as `z`, and melee requires equal height.
- Maps may include `objects`: up to 256 unique ground `{x, y, type}` tiles. Trees and rocks block movement and ranged line of sight; objects cannot overlap walls, gates, elevation, units or the objective. Validation ensures all starting units can still reach the objective. Older maps without objects remain playable.
- Village maps may add up to 32 2–4 tile `houses` rectangles (`left`, `right`, `top`, `bottom`) outside the keep and strongpoints, and `lanes` as unique open, flat `{x, y}` tiles. Houses without a `door` stay solid; an optional perimeter `door: {x, y}` makes all ground-floor tiles enterable, with entry/exit only at that tile. House walls block shots between inside and outside even at the door; melee is possible across the door. Enterable floors display without roofs; higher floors and castles remain future work. Houses provide exterior cover and cannot overlap objects, starting units, elevation, catapults or the objective; lanes change only appearance, not movement costs. Objects additionally accept `fence` (blocks movement but not shots) and `well` (blocks both); both provide adjacent ranged cover. The seeded Greywatch Hamlet game has enterable houses; older maps and active match snapshots remain unchanged.
- Maps may include `strongpoints`: up to 16 non-overlapping rectangles with `left`, `right`, `top`, `bottom` and a `gate: {x, y}` on their perimeter. They cannot overlap the original keep or each other. Their walls block movement and shots, provide cover, and cannot host objects, elevation, units or the objective; all starting units must still reach the objective. Legacy maps omit this field.
- Optional `mission: "capture_king"` maps place exactly one defender with `weapon: null, is_king: true` on an open objective tile strictly inside the keep and at least one other defender in `enemyStartPositions`. The king remains alive and stationary, never receives AI orders or attacks, and cannot be captured while a guard stands adjacent at his height. An adjacent sword-bearing soldier on the same height spends one fight point using the `capture` action to win; killing the king instead loses the mission. Old maps with `type: "king"` remain supported. Maps without `mission` retain courtyard capture rules.
- Optional `catapults: [{"x": 3, "y": 7}]` places up to 16 siege engines on distinct open ground tiles (not the objective). A selected attacker standing on one may use `aim_catapult` (`unit_id`, `catapult_id`, `x`, `y`) to spend one fight point on a target 2–10 tiles away, and `fire_catapult` (`unit_id`, `catapult_id`) to spend another fight point and one of three stones when reloaded. Reload takes two turn transitions; aim persists between turns. A shot hits a unit (including an ally or king) on the target tile for two damage, or damages a wall tile; two wall hits permanently breach it for movement, line of sight, cover, rendering and AI. State holds `catapults`, `wall_damage`, and `breaches`; old maps/states without these fields still work. Projectiles are transient response events.
- Catapult impacts also leave persistent `smoke: [{x, y, turns}]` clouds for two turn transitions. A repeat impact refreshes the same tile. Smoke blocks line of sight, including shots from or into the cloud, but not movement or melee; it does not affect damage on the impact that created it. The server filters clouds on fogged snapshots by current visibility, and the renderer displays their remaining duration. Old matches without `smoke` remain valid.
- A catapult impact on authored grass or an enterable wooden house floor also ignites `fire: [{x, y, turns}]` for three transitions. At end turn it spreads to neighboring grass or floors within the same house, but never reignites spent fuel in `burned` unless struck by another stone. Fire blocks sight like smoke, adds 2 MP on entry and deals 1 damage on entry or at turn transition; armor and horses absorb it first. Soldiers on or beside a reachable burning tile can spend one fight point on `extinguish_fire` (`unit_id`, `x`, `y`). Fogged API and defender AI hide unseen fire; old matches without `fire` or `burned` still work.
- Optional `items: [{"x": 2, "y": 6, "type": "healing_potion"}]` places up to 32 potions on distinct walkable ground tiles (including enterable houses). State holds uniquely identified items with `carrier_id` or ground `x`/`y`. Attackers can carry up to two potions, using one fight point per `pickup_item`, `drop_item`, or `use_item` action. A potion heals up to two health on self or an adjacent ally at the same height through a legal doorway, and is consumed; carried potions drop at a casualty's tile. The seeded Hamlet has two potions. Existing instances without `items` remain playable.
- A casualty drops their weapon, armor and carried tools as distinct uniquely identified ground equipment alongside carried potions (even if armor was removed). Authored `items` can also include `{x, y, type: "weapon", name: "sword" | "longbow" | "crossbow"}`, `{x, y, type: "armor", name: "leather" | "mail"}`, or `{x, y, type: "tool", name: "rope"}` on distinct walkable ground tiles, within the shared 32-item limit. An attacker may `drop_weapon`, `drop_armor`, or `pickup_equipment` with a ground `item_id`, one fight point each. Recovery requires the matching slot empty and the resulting load ≤8; recovered armor is worn immediately without resetting protection already spent that turn. `pickup_tool` with `item_id` and `drop_tool` with `tool_index` cost one fight point, require room for up to two carried labels and an eight-weight cap, and preserve spent MP. A carried rope permits one-level unaccessed elevation steps on foot with ordinary terrain MP costs; its effect ends when dropped. Other tool effects remain future work. Dropping/recovering gear updates current MP without undoing points spent; a `next_item_id` counter avoids reused IDs, including on older saved matches. Potion actions remain potion-only. Fogged snapshots hide gear and tools on unseen tiles.
- Starting roster units can include `armor: "leather"` or `"mail"`; armor starts equipped and is carried even when removed. Leather weighs 1 and absorbs the first 1 damage each turn; mail weighs 3 and absorbs 2. A hit blocked by cover does not spend armor protection. The same per-turn protection applies to melee, ranged and unit-targeted catapult hits, for both sides. A selected attacker spends one fight point on `equip_armor` with `equipped: true/false`; changing equipment does not replenish spent protection. Missing armor on legacy maps and active matches defaults to none. The untouched seeded Hamlet demonstrates both armor types.
- Carried weight includes weapons (sword 1, longbow 2, crossbow 3), armor (even removed), tools (1 each) and carried potions (1 each). Each 3 weight reduces movement allowance by 1 MP, with at least 1 MP per turn; at most 8 weight and 2 potions can be carried. Picking up, dropping or using potions and equipment adjusts current-turn MP while preserving points already spent. The API supplies `carried_weight`, `max_carry_weight`, `weight_penalty` and weight-aware `next_move_capacity` per unit; route preview uses these server-computed current/future allowances. Legacy matches without stored move capacity are supported.
- Optional `terrain: [{"x": 5, "y": 7, "type": "road"}]` assigns outdoor ground tiles a foot movement cost on entry: road 1, grass 2, mud 3, swamp 4 MP. Unspecified tiles (including old maps) cost 1 MP; lanes remain purely visual. Typed tiles must be distinct, open, and non-elevated. Pathfinding and defender goals use cheapest total MP, and a unit cannot enter a tile whose cost exceeds its per-turn allowance or spend part of a step across turns. Previews display the actual MP cost and exact turn boundary, accounting for weight and occupancy.
- Optional `horses: [{"x": 2, "y": 6}]` spawns up to 16 horses on distinct open, flat outdoor tiles (including a starting soldier's tile), not on the objective or in swamp. A soldier on an unmounted horse may spend one fight point to mount; dismounting costs one fight point and leaves the horse on that tile. A mounted soldier has 6 base MP without load penalties, retains morale/rank bonuses, and sword melee deals 2 damage. Mounted movement uses road 1, grass 2, mud 4, unmarked ground 1 MP; swamps, houses and ladders are impassable, while ramps remain usable. A mounted soldier cannot crew a catapult. Incoming hits strike the horse first (2 HP); horse death dismounts the surviving rider and does not change morale. Active old matches without `horses` or `mount_id` remain playable. The untouched seeded Hamlet includes a horse under its first soldier; existing matches and customized maps are not rewritten.
- Optional `fog_of_war: true` enables shared six-tile daylight line of sight for attacker and defender teams. The engine saves explored terrain after each action and checks that attack targets are currently visible. API snapshots omit hidden defenders and their roster start positions, ground supplies and unwitnessed combat events; they expose only currently visible and previously explored tile lists, not persistent enemy locations. The defender AI sees only attackers its side can currently spot. A separate seeded Reconnaissance game demonstrates fog; old maps without the flag keep full visibility.
- Optional `night: true` requires fog of war. Units see unlit ground within two Manhattan steps; a lit carried `torch` grants its bearer five-step sight, but exposes the bearer's tile to enemies with clear sight within six steps. A soldier with a torch can spend one fight point on `toggle_torch` (`unit_id`, `lit: true/false`); torches in older matches default to lit, and dropping the last torch resets its light state for future pickup. Optional `watchfires: [{x, y}]` light tiles within three steps for both teams (up to 16 distinct open outdoor tiles); observers still need a clear line of sight and must be within six steps. A soldier on or beside a reachable watchfire at the same height can spend one fight point to extinguish or relight it; `extinguished_watchfires` persists independently of the authored map, and old matches default to all fires lit. On fog maps, direct attacks (including reactions) deal +1 damage when the target's team cannot see the attacker; cover and armor still apply, catapult impacts are excluded, and defenders only attack visible targets. The seeded Night Watch scenario demonstrates night sight without altering old matches.
- Attackers may `reserve_reaction` with `unit_id` and boolean `reserved` to set aside or reclaim one fight point before ending the turn. An armed, ready soldier automatically strikes once when a defender moves into legal melee or ranged range and is visible; interrupts resolve after each movement step, before defender attacks, and use ordinary damage, cover, armor and reload rules. Unused reservations expire at the end of the defender turn; older units without `reaction_reserved` default to unreserved. The selected-soldier control highlights eligible trigger tiles.
- Attackers may `reserve_movement` with `unit_id` and boolean `reserved` to hold or reclaim all remaining MP. After each defender movement step and any attack reaction, a soldier with reserved MP automatically evades once if that defender is visible and adjacent at the same height through a legal melee connection. The escape uses one unoccupied, legal cardinal step that increases the distance from that defender and costs no more than the reserved MP; terrain and mounted restrictions apply. The selected-soldier control highlights potential trigger tiles in blue. Unused MP and reservations expire at turn end, and legacy units without `move_reserved` default to zero.
- An unmounted attacker without reserved movement may `crouch` with `unit_id`, spending one fight point and all remaining MP. Crouching prevents movement, mounting and movement reservations until the end of the defender turn; incoming direct ranged shots deal one less damage before armor, but melee and catapult hits are unchanged. Existing cover still blocks shots outright. The stance expires when the next attacker turn begins; older states without `crouched` default to standing.
- A carried `shield` tool (weight 1, occupying a tool slot) enables `raise_shield` with `unit_id` and boolean `raised`. Raising costs one FP and one MP on foot without a movement reserve or crouch; lowering is free. While raised, direct melee/ranged attacks from currently visible enemies lose one damage per hit before armor; hidden attackers, cover-blocked shots, horse hits and catapults bypass it. Shield sight shrinks by two daylight/torch tiles or one dark tile; existing shared sight from other soldiers and watchfire illumination still apply. The stance ends next turn or when the last shield is dropped. Older states default to unraised. The seeded Shieldwall scenario demonstrates the trade-off on a fog map.
- Optional `exit: {"x": 2, "y": 6}` defines an open, flat outdoor tile distinct from the objective and reachable by every attacker. A soldier on the exit can use `withdraw` with `unit_id` for one fight point, leaving the field with their carried items, gear and mounted horse. State stores survivor snapshots in `withdrawn`; when no attackers remain on the field, at least one evacuee wins by retreat, otherwise the mission is defeated. Courtyard and king objectives continue to work. The victory panel shows evacuees, fallen soldiers and evacuated equipment; villagers and prisoners are future work. Legacy maps without an exit behave as before.
- A survivor reduced to exactly 1 HP by a hit receives a two-turn `wound_turns` injury, losing 1 MP on foot (minimum 1 MP). Hits fully blocked by armor, cover or a horse do not wound the rider. Wounds decrement at each side's next turn start; a healing potion clears an injury immediately, including when the patient already has full HP. Movement adjusts without restoring spent points, and the API's next-turn allowance accounts for impending recovery. Old unit snapshots without `wound_turns` default to uninjured. Persistent campaign injuries remain future work.

## Local workflow

- Use Python 3.12 and the dependencies in `requirements.txt`. Run `make venv`, `make migrate`, and `make run` to serve the game at `http://127.0.0.1:8098/`.
- Run `make createsuperuser` to create an editor login, then open `/admin/`. Save a single game with a reachable objective before linking it to a scenario; only available linked games can be launched.
- Run `.venv/bin/python3.12 manage.py test game` for engine and API tests; use `node --test game/tests/test_playback.cjs` for browser interaction tests and `node --check` on the browser scripts to check syntax.
- When changing map geometry, update simulation walkability, pathfinding expectations, rendering, and pointer-to-tile behavior together. When changing catalog fields, update models, migrations, API serialization, UI, and tests together.
- Keep original placeholder art and any added assets' attribution/license information intact. Do not treat `.venv/`, `.idea/`, or the local `db.sqlite3` as source files to edit.
- The settings are development-only; do not rely on them for production deployment or put credentials in code or documentation.
